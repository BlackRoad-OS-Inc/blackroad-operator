## Slide 11

Customer Journey Mapping (CJM) Overview

Definition: process of visualizing how a customer interacts with a business by mapping out the actions they take to achieve a goal. Customer journey mapping outlines key events, customer motivations, and areas of friction within their experience. This info becomes a comprehensive visual that describes a customer’s typical experience with your business.

Illustrates the interaction between digital and traditional channels/touchpoints which should harmonize.

In practice it’s rarely linear as customers interact with several touchpoints as they decide.

A single CJM focuses on the journey for one persona.


## Slide 13

Why It Matters

Your digital communications strategy should consider all the touchpoints that a customer or prospective customer may interact with.

A touchpoint in a customer journey map is an instance where your customer can form an opinion of your business. They’re everywhere a customer or prospect comes into direct or indirect contact with your business.

This includes your website, social media, search engine marketing, Google/Yelp reviews, potential digital customer service, emails.

It’s important that the different types of touchpoints are considered in your customer journey map because they can help uncover opportunities for improvement in the buying journey.


## Slide 14

How to create a CJM

Start with a goal.

Select a persona to focus on.

List out all the touchpoints.

What are they thinking/feeling? How can you ensure the journey is smooth and frictionless?

What are the customer’s actions/main priorities?

What are the customer’s pain points? How might you mitigate them? You may ask your customer service team about the most common complaints/issues they hear about.

What do we need to do to move the customer along to the next step – from awareness to consideration to consideration? How should we be communicating with the customer during each stage?

You may ask your customers directly what it’s like doing business with your company so you have a more accurate snapshot of the customer’s journey.

Take the customer journey yourself, put yourself in the shoes of this persona.

Determine the resources you have and the ones you’ll need.

You may not be able to fix/control everything. For example, if the price is too high, perhaps you realize that you shouldn’t focus on this persona since they aren’t able to afford your product.

Make the necessary changes to your digital strategy and execution to improve the overall customer experience.

Review/update it regularly as new products/services or changing circumstances may change the CJM.

Share it broadly with the relevant communications and product teams.


## Slide 15

Types of CJMs

Current state: illustrates the actions, thoughts, and emotions your customers currently experience while interacting with your company. Most frequently used.

Day in the life: captures the persona’s entire day, whether or not that includes interacting with your brand or product. They are best used for addressing unmet customer needs before customers even know they exist. You can use this info to develop new products/services or better communication strategies.

Future state: These customer journey maps visualize what actions, thoughts, and emotions your customers will experience in future interactions with your company.  Best used for planning.

Service blueprint: the most complex, takes an existing CJM  and layers on the factors responsible for delivering that experience, including people, policies, technologies, and processes. They are best used for identifying the root causes of current customer journeys or identifying the steps needed to attain desired future customer journeys.


## Slide 16

Benefits of a CJM

Find your sweet spot: In looking at the needs and pain points of your various personas, you’ll be able to identify which personas’ needs are best met by your product and you can focus your digital strategy efforts there.

Implement a proactive customer service strategy: This will help you maintain a strong brand reputation.

Improve your customer retention and conversion rates: By strategically addressing pain points and understanding which communications levers to pull to move people to the next stage in the buying process, you’ll improve overall ROI.

Create a customer first mentality throughout the company: Ensure that your goals are compatible with what customers really need vs. what you think they need or how your team’s success is being measured.


## Slide 17

What is a SWOT Analysis?


## Slide 18

Strengths – Internal Positives

In your control, should emphasize and optimize. What do you do well?

Are you resource rich?

Do people already know and love your brand? Or you do have an amazing plan so that people soon will?

Do you have a lot of engaged social media followers/brand advocates or a large number website visitors each month?

Is your product or service more popular/better than the competition? Do you offer something they don’t?


## Slide 19

Weaknesses – Internal Negatives

You can control and improve these things.

What do competitors do better?

What digital areas needs to improve?

Are your ads being ignored?

What resources are you lacking (time, people, tools, budget)? Inferior products?


## Slide 20

Opportunities – External Positives

Not in your control but you can still take advantage of them.

What does your audience need that the marketplace is not providing where you could step in?

What could you do better than anyone else?

How is your industry/culture-trends/circumstances changing in a positive way that you could take advantage of?


## Slide 21

Threats – External Negatives

Things beyond your control that you’ll need to adapt to.

These current or emerging changes can be in the political, economic, social, or technological environment which can directly affect your product and how it is viewed in the market.

Is there increased competition? Is your product becoming obsolete or irrelevant?

Are there more regulations?


## Slide 22

Why It Matters

It helps you to think short-term and long-term. It can help you in identifying an opportunity right on time and exploiting it for your business advantage.

By leveraging SWOT analysis, you can create a better and more strategic plan to meet your objectives and adapt yourself to the changing market conditions.

It also helps your business analyze your strengths which helps you know what to emphasize in content development and how to best reach desired targets.

It lets you understand the positive and negative impacts on your business. And which ones you’re able to control/fix.
