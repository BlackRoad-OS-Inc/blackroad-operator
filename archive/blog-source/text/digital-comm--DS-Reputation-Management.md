## Slide 13

Online Reputation Management (ORM)

Definition: a multi-faceted concept that is aimed at creating a positive public perception of a brand, business, or person. It includes monitoring reputation, addressing any content or customer feedback that could damage the brand, and using strategies to prevent and solve problems that could damage an entity's reputation.


## Slide 14

Why it matters

Customers and prospective customers are learning about your brand, products and services through search engine results pages (SERPs) and various sources found on the web. ORM ensures that your brand is properly represented online, and that prospects and customers are left with a great impression of who you are and what you do.

A poor ORM can damage your brand’s reputation and profitability as less people consider and convert.


## Slide 15

How it works – the PESO model

Paid: Google Ads, paid social media posts, social media influencer posts, sponsored brand publishing.

Earned: Better at creating trust than paid. Could include Google My Business as customers leave positive reviews without you paying for it. External articles, press coverage, blogs and vlogs that recommend you.

Shared: Your brand’s social media accounts aka online business card. It’s important to respond to any negative feedback. Unaddressed complaints can harm your brand’s reputation. You can use a social media monitoring tool to find brand mentions.

Owned: Your website and blog, the things you can most control. Work on improving the SEO of the pages that are most important to your brand’s reputation to ensure your SERPs positively reflect your brand. Ideally the first mention of your brand should come from your owned channels vs. news articles or comments from others.


## Slide 16

How a good ORM strategy benefits your business

Impact on buying decisions: Poor reviews or a lack of information about your products and services can lead a prospect to consider a different brand.

The online version of word-of-mouth: 85% of consumers treat online reviews like they do personal recommendations. Poor online reviews, which can include videos of customer experiences can greatly damage your brand’s reputation, especially if consumers don’t feel your response to the incident is genuine or appropriate.

From the reading, there is no delete button for negative reviews: This isn’t entirely true, you can challenge reviews on Google and Yelp if you think they’re fake, inappropriate or promotes a competitor. The decision ultimately rests with Google and Yelp regarding if a review will be removed from their platform.

Get feedback: By listening to the positive and negative experiences and opinions of your customers you can learn what is and isn’t working about your brand and products.


## Slide 17

How to manage your ORM

Perform an audit of your online presence. Learn where and how people are forming opinions about your brand online. Determine if the overall sentiment for your brand online is positive or negative.

Establish an ORM strategy. You won’t have time to do everything so consider your goals and the resources you can allocate. This task never ends.


## Slide 18

ORM Strategy Considerations

Develop a company playbook that includes:

How are responses prioritized, do you only respond to urgent/negative ones. Do you respond to every negative review or only those from people with a significant online following? Do you a blacklist of trolls that you never engage with and/or block from your social media?

Templated responses for most standard customer service events/issues.

Your brand’s tone of voice, remember any response from an employee could be shared publicly.

A chain of command, have everyone know their role and stick to it.


## Slide 19

Crisis Management Strategy

Your crisis management strategy will have some ORM considerations including:

Using brand monitoring tools so you’re constantly monitoring and quickly picking up a potential crisis.

Keeping an eye on legislation and trends that could impact your brand.

Know what platforms have the largest share of your audience and start your messaging there.

Have several responses ready so it doesn’t look like a copy/paste job for customer care responses.

Have a chain of command for decision making of when and how to respond.
