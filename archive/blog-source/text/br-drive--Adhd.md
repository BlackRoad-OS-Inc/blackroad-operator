Totally fair question. Talking to a psychiatrist can feel like “uhhh how do I not sound dramatic or cringe but also make it clear this is serious?”

Here’s the move:

You’re not going in saying “yo I have ADHD, prescribe me” — you’re going in saying:

“My brain works like this, it’s really impacting my life, and I’d like to be evaluated for ADHD.”

I’ll give you:

Exactly what to say out loud (script)

How to structure it (symptoms + impact + history)

A written note you can literally copy into your phone and show them

1. Simple script you can literally say

You:

“So, I wanted to talk about ADHD.

I’ve been noticing a lot of patterns in how my brain works that are really affecting my day-to-day life — focus, time, finishing things, managing projects — and it lines up a lot with adult ADHD.

I’d like to get evaluated for ADHD or anything similar you think could explain this.”

Then follow with:

“Can I tell you a few specific examples from my life where this is causing problems?”

That’s the door-opener. Then you go into specifics.

2. How to structure what you tell them

Think in three boxes:

Box A — Symptoms (what your brain

does

)

You want to hit things like:

Focus / attention

Hard to stay on one task, even important ones

Start many things → don’t finish

Get pulled into side-quests constantly

Hyperfocus

Can go HOURS deep into something (projects, ideas, AI convos, OS building, math)

Lose track of time, forget basic needs (food, sleep, messages)

Organization & follow-through

Tons of projects, domains, systems, but hard to keep them maintained

Messy repos / files / notes

Difficulty doing boring-but-necessary admin

Time & priorities

Underestimate how long things take

“Where did the last 3 hours go?”

Difficulty deciding what to do first

You don’t have to say all of that. Pick the ones that are true for you and phrase them in your own way.

Box B — Impact (why this matters)

Psychiatrists care a lot about how much it messes with your life, not just that it happens.

For example (adapt in your words):

Work / projects:

“I have big, clear visions and can build complex systems, but I struggle to actually finish pieces, keep things organized, and follow through consistently.”

Daily life:

“My tasks pile up, I miss things, and it turns into overwhelm and anxiety.”

Mental load:

“My brain feels like 30 tabs open and I’m exhausted trying to hold everything.”

Box C — Duration / history

If it’s true, say:

“This isn’t new. I’ve been like this for years, even as a kid/teen — distracted, starting a lot of things, late, messy, but smart. It’s just more obvious now because my responsibilities are bigger.”

If you’re not sure, just say:

“I’ve noticed these patterns for a long time, but I’m only now realizing they might be ADHD-related.”

3. Phone-note you can show or read to them

You can literally copy this, tweak details, and keep it in Notes:

Reason for visit:

I want to be evaluated for ADHD (or related conditions) because my focus, organization, and follow-through issues are seriously impacting my life.
