## Slide 4

Digital Strategy – Today’s Trend: Ad Frequency

Ad Frequency is the average number of times your ad is displayed to a unique user. It is calculated by dividing your ad's impressions by its reach. This metric helps understand how frequently your ad is viewed by your target audience on average.

According to Facebook, the optimal ad frequency is between 1 and 2 exposures during the length of the campaign with a tipping point of 3.4, after which an ad loses its effectiveness.


## Slide 5

What to do if your ad frequency is too high:

Set up frequency rules

Stop targeting uninterested people

Use geotargeting and nanotargeting

Run shorter, more memorable campaigns

Adjust your budget


## Slide 6

Digital Strategy and Digital Campaign – what’s the difference?

Oftentimes, these two terms are used interchangeably; however, they are not the same thing. The digital marketing strategy is your plan of action—the steps you have laid out that you need to follow to reach your goal. The digital marketing campaign is the actual actions taking place. So basically, the strategy is the “talk” and the campaign is the “walk”.

A career in strategic communications will ask you to work on both aspects and we’ll cover concepts under both terms in the class and in your assignments.


## Slide 7

Steps in crafting a digital marketing strategy:

#1: Determine your goals and how you’ll measure them – what do you hope to achieve? How will you know if you achieved it?

#2: Determine your target audience – who do you want to reach?

#3: Decide on your platforms or channels – where will you reach them?

#4: Create and post content

#5: Monitor, evaluate and adjust your strategy throughout the lifecycle of your effort


## Slide 8

Step #1 Goal setting (use for your 1st assignment):

Goals could focus on awareness/interest, consideration or conversion/purchase.

Develop quantitative goals vs. qualitative goals.

Qualitative: “We will sell more Nike shoes”

Quantitative: “We will have $100 million in sales of Men’s Nike One Waffle shoes in Q4 ’21.”

Set S.M.A.R.T. goals.

## Slide 10

How do you know if your digital strategy worked?

It worked if you met your goal. If you didn’t meet your goal, take time to try and figure out what did and didn’t work.

Were you on the right channels/platforms?

Was your content not as interesting, educational, helpful as it needed to be?

Were you targeting the right audience?

You can also compare it to a benchmark.

If you’ve run several different campaigns, you’ll have a lot of data that you can compare your effort to. For example, if your goal was to drive consideration and drive people to a website, you can compare the Click Through Rate of your effort to a previous campaign/digital strategy. Setting benchmarks takes time but is another good way to demonstrate the ROI (return on investment) of what you spent on your digital strategy.


## Slide 11

Step #2: Determine your target audience

Before you decide where you target people (channel/platform) and what you create, you need to determine who you want to reach.

Will you target existing customers (consideration/conversion) or prospects (awareness/consideration) or both?

A digital strategy may have more than one target audience.

You’ll want to start by analyzing your market. Determine whether you’re operating in a well-established market or developing one. Is your industry mainstream? Who are your major competitors?

From there, are you going to copy their audiences or look for unique ones that aren’t currently being reached?


## Slide 12

Step #2: Research the competition deep dive

As you begin to develop content and your target audiences, visit the channels of your competitors. Follow their social, sign up for their emails.

Evaluate what platforms they are most active on and what content is and isn’t performing for them.

You may decide to focus on the networks where your audience is underserved, rather than trying to win fans away from a dominant player on a particular platform.

Use Hootsuite or other social listening software to monitor your competitors, key industry words and hashtags. Look for content trends that you can capitalize on and popular content types that you can build on.

Develop a unique brand tone and voice that will make your content stand out.


## Slide 13

Step #2: Determine your target audience

Consider the following questions:

Who is your typical customer?

How old are they?

What is their income and education level?

What is their geography – does weather or climate impact their need for your product? Is it seasonal? If it’s seasonal, are there complimentary products for other seasons?

What are they interested in outside of your product and service?

What types of media do they consume?

## Slide 16

Determine your platform

It is not necessary to be on every platform and managing unique content for each platform can be time consuming.

Develop a specific goal that articulates why you are on a platform and what you hope to achieve there.

If you don’t have a specific reason for being on a platform, consider not having a presence or waiting until you have established success on fewer platforms.

While each platform may have unique content, your brand tone, voice and style should feel consistent so people know what to expect from your brand.


## Slide 17

Step #4 Create and Post Content

We’ll do a content deep dive as we learn about each of the various channels in turn.

You’ll develop a content calendar that includes everything that you plan to post. Use the 80/20 rule to start:

80 percent of your posts should inform, educate, or entertain your audience

20 percent can directly promote your brand.

You can adjust this ratio and include more branded content if you find that your audience is receptive to brand messages.


## Slide 18

Step #5 Monitor, evaluate and adjust your strategy throughout the lifecycle

Your digital strategy will continually evolve as your goals change.

You can use in-platform analytics and reporting and UTM or other parameters to track users as they move from digital channels to conversions.

Continually test different types of content on different platforms. In the paid space, test many different audiences to see which ones are most receptive.
