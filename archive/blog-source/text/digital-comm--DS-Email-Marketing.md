## Slide 15

Email Marketing

Typically targets your existing customer base to increase conversions

Possible goals:

Build relationships: You can use artificial intelligence and customer data to personalize emails to consumers specific needs. The more detailed you get, the more emails you’ll send per campaign.

Boost brand awareness: Keep your brand and your services top-of-mind for the moment when your prospects are ready to engage.

Promote your content: Use email to share relevant blog content or useful assets with your prospects.

Generate leads: Entice subscribers to provide their personal information in exchange for an asset that they’d find valuable.

Market your products: Promote your products and services.

Nurture leads: Share content that can help them succeed in their goals. More frequently done in the B2B space since CTR are 47% higher in for B2B.


## Slide 16

Benefits

More effective than social media at driving conversions.

You control the email environment.

20% of people you’re emailing are qualified leads vs. cold calling on social media or paid search.


## Slide 17

Steps in your email strategy

Define your audience

Establish your goal and KPIs for deliverability, open rates, click through rates and unsubscribes. This varies by industry and age of target. Unsubscribes have increased now due to AI tools.

Create a way for people to sign up. This can be done via gathering emails at physical store checkout, through a form on your website or through a lead gen campaign on social media.

Choose a type of email campaign type and segment your email lists. Each brand will likely have several different types of email campaigns and within a campaign have several emails for where a prospect/customer is in their customer journey or product lifecycle.

Make a schedule.

Send emails.

Measure your results.


## Slide 18

How to send emails

Choose an email marketing service like Mailchimp

Create your email. Have a clear call to action, ensure your images and videos are mobile responsive, make your subject line actionable.

Incorporate email automation such as autoresponders where each step in the journey triggers the next email. Or workflows which know more about the actions a person has already taken.

Use email marketing templates to save your team time. Always send a test email for each new email form to yourself and check it on a laptop and mobile device before sending it out.


## Slide 19

A/B Testing

Choose one variable to test at a time, e.g., subject line, CTA, images.

Create two versions of the email: one with and one without the variable.

Allow your emails to be sent out simultaneously for a period of time.

Analyze your results and keep only the version that performed better.

Test a new variable and repeat the process.


## Slide 20

Types of emails

Informational emails: only send to people who have opted in to hear about new products, upcoming sales, etc.

Product update emails: not as well received, should be sent sparingly.

Digital magazine or newsletter: Select the top highlights from monthly or weekly content to send to your subscribers.

Event invitations: must emphasize what’s in it for the attendee as people receive too many of these.


## Slide 21

Types of emails

Dedicated send: Sent only to a small group of people will extra relevant information.

Social media emails: for example, LinkedIn InMails

Internal updates: These tend to have higher open rates as employees/students want to know the info.

Confirmation emails: The extra reassurance purchasers want and need.

Form submission emails: an extra place to store the “gift” someone got for giving you their info.
