## Slide 6

THEORIES OF RELATIONSHIPS:

Systems Theory – organization and the environment are in a relationship and need one another – different parts relate.

actions of one part affect the others.

OPEN SYSTEMS -  Use Public Relations to find out how they are doing with stakeholders. They bring in information. Public relations folks are the go-between.

CLOSED SYSTEMS – Do NOT seek new information. Makes decisions based on the history of the organization.


## Slide 7

THEORIES OF RELATIONSHIPS:

Situation Theory – Publics are not all the same – some seek information actively and some passively receive – different communication needs. People will act when they believe a situation affects them personally or their actions can make a difference.

Publics are situational – that is the key for situation theory.

Problem recognition – must be aware of the issue and see it can influence them.

Constraint recognition – how obstacles are perceived; can they do something.

Level of involvement – how much they care; they must care about resolving it.

Helps the practitioner predict when groups will become active or remain apathetic.

Helps the practitioner create communication strategies for specific publics.


## Slide 8

Theories Of Persuasion


## Slide 9

THEORIES OF PERSUASION:

Persuasion  - The use of communication in an attempt to shape, change, and/or reinforce perception, affect (feelings), cognition (thoughts) and/or behavior.

Awareness – accepting information for the first time.

Attitudes – predispositions to like or dislike things

Beliefs – assessments that things are true or false

Behavior – observable actions


## Slide 10

THEORIES OF PERSUASION:

SOCIAL EXCHANGE THEORY – People want to keep costs low and rewards high. Example – respond to survey – keep it short, etc. (my Disney example).

DIFFUSION THEORY – People adopt an idea only after going through five discrete stages (Awareness, interest, evaluation, trial, adoption). See next slide

SOCIAL LEARNING THEORY – We can learn by watching others, and if their behavior is rewarded, then the more you are likely to do it. We decide what to do based on what we anticipate the reward is.


## Slide 11

Diffusion Theory – The Five Stages Of Adoption

1.	Awareness – Topic known but knowledge limited.

Stage engages the mass media and significant others

2. 	Interest – Development of interest begins; information sought.

Stage engages the mass media and significant others

3.	Evaluation – Idea applied to individual situations, more information obtained.

Stage engages unbiased third parties and significant others

4. 	Trial – Use begins on a small scale.

Stage engages unbiased third parties and significant others

5.	Adoption – Idea, service or product adopted after being proven worthwhile.

Stage engages significant others and personal experience


## Slide 12

Persuasion Theories

Functional Models of Attitudes – If an attitude about a product is based on instrumental qualities, show the product qualities (mileage on a car). If an attitude about a product is based on image qualities, show the image (cool visuals of the car racing through streets.

Cognitive Dissonance – Resolve inconsistent belief system – Somebody smokes; They know that smoking causes cancer. To resolve the dissonance they have to change their behavior or their belief (well, it only happens in severe cases; I know lots of people perfectly healthy who still smoke).  In ads, people prefer information that is consistent with their views of themselves.


## Slide 13

Persuasion Theories

Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA) – decisions on 	behavior are based on ATTITUDES and NORMS.

Attitudes – what you think about buying the product 	(not about the product itself, but about buying it).

Norms – what you think other people think about it.

Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) – elaboration of 	TRA

and adds a third factor – Perceived Behavioral Control 	(efficacy) – can you control this?  Ex: can you afford to 	buy the product?

## Slide 15

The Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)

Central route persuasion: occurs when involvement is high

Persuasion is more lasting

Persuasion is influenced by argument strength

Peripheral route persuasion: occurs when involvement is low

Persuasion through this route is temporary and not strong

Persuasion is influenced by irrelevant cues (e.g. humor, imagery, spokespeople, music, etc.)

Theories Of Media


## Slide 17

THEORIES OF MEDIA:

USES AND GRATIFICATIONS THEORY – People use the media in

different ways and for different reasons (not everybody heard news 	about your company). See next slide

FRAMING THEORY – Messages from the media carry 	FRAMES or

preexisting interpretations. This is useful. If you want to be 	understood, frame the story in a way the audience understands (i.e. 	freedom, war on terror, individual effort)

AGENDA SETTING THEORY – The media does not tell us what to 	think. They tell us what to think about. The media can build 	awareness and increase salience.

MEDIA DEPENDENCY THEORY – The media tell us what to think if we 	have no other information.


## Slide 18

Uses And Gratifications Theory

People are active users of media and choose how and when to use media based on its gratification for them.

Reasons for media usage include…

for entertainment

to scan the environment for items that are important to them

for a diversion

for a substitute for personal relationships

for a check on self-identity

The uses and gratifications theory helps the practitioner explain media effects, or the absence of effects.

The practitioner must remember that just because a message is available doesn’t mean that people will pay attention and remember it.

## Slide 32

PR Roles - The Manager

Roles have been defined in public relations theory by public relations scholars Glen Broom and David Dozier

Expert prescriber: person who operates as a consultant to define the problem, suggest options, and oversee implementation

Communication facilitator: person on the boundary between the organization and its environment who keeps two-way communication flowing.

Problem-solving facilitator: person who partners with senior management to identify and solve problems.
