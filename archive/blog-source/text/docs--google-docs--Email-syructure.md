Product Email Structuring Questions

1. Hook & Client Situation (Engage the Advisor by Addressing the Client’s Current Reality)*

- Where is the client currently in their financial journey?

- What specific needs or concerns do they have?

- What challenges are they facing? (e.g., retirement planning, market volatility, income gaps, healthcare costs, etc.)

2. Solution Statement & Product Introduction** *(Position the Product as the Answer)*

- What problem does this product help solve?

- How does this product fit into a client’s overall financial strategy?

- What is the product name, and what is its core purpose?

3. Key Highlights & Benefits (Why It Matters to the Client)*

- What are the top 3-5 benefits of this product?

- How does this product impact the client’s financial security, growth, or protection?

- What makes this product different from other options?

#### **4. Features & Facts** *(The Specifics Advisors Need to Know)*

- What key features contribute to the product’s benefits? (e.g., indexed crediting, roll-ups, withdrawal options)

- Who is eligible for this product?

- What are the costs, limits, and other important details advisors need to know?

#### **5. Call to Action** *(Encourage Next Steps)*

- What should the advisor do next?

- How can they request an illustration or discuss client fit?

- Where can they find additional resources or product details?

- How can they contact you for further discussion?

This framework helps ensure every product email is structured effectively, guiding the advisor from identifying a client need to taking action. Let me know if you’d like to refine it further!

That’s a great structure! Here’s an optimized flow for structuring an email about a product:

### **1. Hook & Client Situation** *(Engage the Advisor by Addressing the Client’s Current Reality)*

- Where a client **currently is** (their financial situation, needs, or common scenario).

- The **problem** they’re facing (concerns about retirement, market volatility, healthcare costs, income gaps, etc.).

### **2. Solution Statement & Product Introduction** *(Position the Product as the Answer)*

- A **clear, concise statement** on how the product solves their issue.

- Introduce the **product name** and its **core purpose**.

### **3. Key Highlights & Benefits** *(Why It Matters to the Client)*

- **Top 3-5 benefits** that make this product stand out.

- Focus on **client outcomes** (income security, growth, protection, flexibility).

### **4. Features & Facts** *(The Specifics Advisors Need to Know)*

- **Key features** that drive those benefits (indexed crediting, roll-ups, withdrawal options, etc.).

- **Eligibility, costs, limits**, and other important details.

### **5. Call to Action** *(Encourage Next Steps)*

- How the advisor can **learn more, request an illustration, or discuss client fit**.

- Provide **contact info** and portal resources if applicable.

This structure keeps the email **engaging, problem-solving, and action-driven** while still including the numbers and details advisors need. Let me know if you’d like a sample using this approach!
