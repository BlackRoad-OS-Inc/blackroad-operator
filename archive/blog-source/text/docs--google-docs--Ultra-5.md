How Credit Methods Work Together

Understanding how these crediting methods interact is vital for creating robust investment strategies. Here’s a breakdown of the five crediting methods available with the Ultra 5:

Cap Rate

The cap rate is the maximum interest that can be credited based on the index’s performance. For example, if the cap rate is set at 9%, and the S&P 500 rises by 10%, clients would only receive a credit of 9%. This ensures clients can benefit from market growth while capping their potential gains.

Guaranteed Cap Rate

This feature guarantees a maximum return for a specified period, such as 5 or 7 years. Clients can rely on this predictability, knowing exactly what they can expect in terms of returns over the term.

Participation Rate

The participation rate indicates how much of the index's performance clients can capitalize on. A 50% participation rate means clients will receive half of the index's gains without any upper limit. This allows clients to enjoy unlimited upside potential while maintaining their capital’s safety.

Performance Trigger Rate

With a performance trigger rate, clients are credited interest if the S&P 500 performs positively, even by a small margin. For instance, if the index rises by just $1, clients would receive a pre-established interest credit, ensuring that even minor gains are rewarded.

Inverse Performance Trigger Rate

This unique feature provides a safety net in case of market declines. If the S&P 500 decreases, clients can still receive credited interest based on a specific rate, ensuring that their investment grows even in unfavorable conditions.

Analysis of Ultra 5 Credit Methods

Patricia is considering choosing Securian’s S&P 500 Index, which offers a 9% cap rate. This means that regardless of how well or how poorly the S&P 500 performs, her gains are capped at 9% with this method. 3. Participation Rate:

Choosing a 50% participation rate allows Patricia to benefit from half of the S&P’s gains without a cap. For instance, if the S&P rises by 20%, she would earn a credited interest of 10%, significantly increasing her investment.

4. Performance Trigger Rate:

With a performance trigger rate of 6.5%, if the S&P rises even slightly, Patricia benefits. If the index goes up by just $1, she will see a return of 6.5% for that year.

5. Inverse Performance Trigger Rate:

This rate offers a safety net. If the S&P declines, Patricia will receive a credit of 12.25%. This feature ensures she has a cushion against market downturns, maintaining the growth of her investment even when markets are volatile.
