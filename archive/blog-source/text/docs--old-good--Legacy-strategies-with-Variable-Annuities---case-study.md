A variable annuity can provide for a rich family legacy. The following 
case study shows how the purchase of an annuity could provide a 
multi-generational legacy. 
A case study benefitting four generations¹ 
This hypothetical case study demonstrates how an initial $500,000 purchase of a MultiOption® 
Guide B annuity contract became a $13.9 million lifetime benefit to children, grandchildren 
and great-grandchildren. 
PART 1
Mark, 45 and Kathy, 45
Mark and Kathy have two children, Ben and Sophia, ages 18 and 16
They purchase a MultiOption Guide B variable annuity with an initial payment of $500,000. 
This could be set up as a joint contract or spouse as beneficiary with both options allowing 
for spousal continuation of the contract.
Optional death benefit rider
To help improve contract values as they age, they purchase the Highest Anniversary Value II 
optional death benefit. With this rider, the beneficiary receives the greater of the guaranteed 
minimum death benefit or the highest anniversary value though age 80.
•	Because optional death benefits can be added to an annuity without underwriting up 
to age 75, they may be beneficial to secure a legacy for those who are underinsured or 
uninsurable. 
After 20 years, Mark dies at age 65; Kathy is now 65
•	As joint owner, Kathy continues ownership of the annuity contract with the death benefit rider.
•	Contract value of the annuity with a moderate hypothetical rate of return with Highest 
Anniversary Value II death benefit has grown from the $500,000 initial purchase to $1,307,847.²

LET'S DEFINE
Nonqualified stretch option: The beneficiary of a nonqualified annuity elects annual required distributions 
based on their life expectancy using the lifetime “nonqualified stretch” payout option. The nonqualified 
stretch option allows for tax-deferred growth and income tax control by spreading distributions, and 
subsequently taxes owed on amounts above cost basis, over their lifetime. This option allows the beneficiary 
the flexibility to take additional amounts throughout their lifetime exceeding the required distribution, as 
well as the option to name a successor beneficiary for continued legacy planning.
More advantages for your legacy planning strategy
Predetermined death benefit – Allows the owner to restrict how distributions are made to beneficiaries, 
especially those who lack education or discipline to manage their assets. An owner could elect the 
nonqualified stretch option to provide lifetime income to their beneficiary while using tax-deferral for 
continued growth. 
Tax-free 1035 exchange – Once the beneficiary is the new owner of the contract, they may be able to 
conduct a tax-free 1035 exchange into a new annuity contract. They can look at the market to see if an 
annuity with features appropriate for their current investment and legacy goals is available. This offers 
owners more protection on their investment as the financial landscape changes in the years to come.
