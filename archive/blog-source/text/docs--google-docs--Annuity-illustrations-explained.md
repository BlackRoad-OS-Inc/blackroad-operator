# Annuity illustrations explained

**Source:** google-docs

---

The first scenario assumes that the historical performance of the index in the most recent 10-year period repeats.

The second scenario is the “low” scenario. In this case, the illustrated index returns are based on the 10-year period, from the last 20 years, which resulted in the least index value growth.

The third scenario, the “high scenario,” is the same as the low scenario except that the 10-year period resulting in the most index value growth is used.

Product illustrations assume that the current cap, participation rate, trigger rate, or other index rates stay constant in the projection. The reality is that the rates are only guaranteed for an initial period (often one year), and they are reset on a regular basis thereafter.

illustrations typically include the following elements.

1. Premium amount: The amount of money you pay to purchase the annuity.

2. Payout options: The different options for how the annuity will pay out, including single life, joint life and period certain.

3. Guaranteed interest rate: The guaranteed minimum interest rate that will be credited to your annuity.

4. Rate of return: The projected rate of return on your annuity based on the investments’ performance.

5. Fees and charges: This can include surrender charges, administrative fees, and mortality and expense charges.

6. Death benefit: The benefit paid to your beneficiaries in the event of your death.

annual liquidity – i.e., how much you can withdraw from your annuity penalty-free.

Inputs: Initial investment, additional contributions, interest rates, and specific terms.

Projections: Expected payouts after the accumulation phase ends.

Scenarios: Economic conditions showing potential growth impacts.
