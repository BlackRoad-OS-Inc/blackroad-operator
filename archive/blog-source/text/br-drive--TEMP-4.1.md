Implicit memory measures

They measure whether people’s performance on a seemingly unrelated task  has improved as a result of having experienced a prior event.

For example, people are NOT asked to recollect the words or ads they have  previously seen, but they are asked to report the first word or brand that  comes to mind.

Thus, implicit measures do not explicitly refer to a prior experience and do  not require conscious or intentional recollection of this experience
