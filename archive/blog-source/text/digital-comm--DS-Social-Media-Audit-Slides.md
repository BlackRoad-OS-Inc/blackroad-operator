## Slide 11

Social Media Audits

Definition: It is a snapshot of all social media activity in and around a brand evaluated for strategic insights.

Allows you to see if there’s synergy between your various social media channels and pieces of content.

It can help you:

Realize the need for increased integration with other departments.

Find gaps in brand promise and product/service performance.

Uncover inconsistencies across brand social accounts.

Reveal blind spots in current social action with content, schedule and response.

Discover consumer ideas for product/service improvements.

Optimize brand content to drive engagement.

Find unexpected consumer generated content on other platforms.

Discover valuable brand or industry influencers.

Optimize time devoted to most effective social media platforms.

Learn from successful competitor social strategies.

Uncover a need for metrics to connect social action to business objectives.

## Slide 13

Analyze the data by asking:

Who—company, consumers, competitors

Where—social media channel (YouTube, Facebook, Pinterest, etc.) and environment (describe the look and feel)

What—type of content (articles, photos, videos, links, questions, etc.) and sentiment (positive, negative, neutral)

When—frequency of activity (number of posts, comments, views, shares, etc. per day, week, or month)

Why—purpose (brand awareness, promotion, drive traffic, customer complaint, praise, etc.)

## Slide 37

Types of social media platforms and formats and how they can help

Audio: Clubhouse, Spotify, Twitter Spaces: Drive high attention and engagement due to listeners opting in to conversations

Long and short form videos: YouTube, TikTok, Instagram Stories and Reels, Facebook Watch: Capture attention, drive brand awareness and show details about your product that a photo can’t capture. Videos should educate, entertain and/or inspire your audience. Purely sales videos won’t engage or drive results.

Disappearing content formats: this one is tricky as more and more platforms are allowing you to not have these disappear. Great for announcements, live events and behind-the-scenes and teaser content. Don’t need to be as polished.

Discussion forms: Reddit, Quora: Be genuinely helpful to your customers by lending your business’ subject matter expertise and answering questions related to your industry.


## Slide 38

Types of social media platforms and formats and how they can help

Shoppable social media platforms and features: Pinterest Product Pins, Facebook Shops, Instagram Shops, TikTok, Shopify: Take advantage of built-in mobile-friendly features to allow your audience to purchase from you without having to leave a social media app. Even if your followers don’t like to make purchases on social media platforms or have longer buyer journeys, shopping features can allow you to tag products, add additional product info and drive traffic to your website.

Social media livestreams: Twitch, YouTube, Instagram Live Rooms, Facebook Live, TikTok: Live video streams can range from one person showing themselves and what they’re doing on their screen to professionally organized panels with multiple speakers. Livestreams also offer the opportunity for users to interact live with the hosts, so it’s vital to monitor and engage with comments during the stream. Facebook also allows you to prerecord and make it look like it’s live.

Closed/private community social media platforms: Discourse, Slack, Facebook Groups: Businesses can use private groups to bring members of their community together to bond over shared challenges, help answer each other’s questions, and feel a sense of professional belonging.


## Slide 39

Content Calendar Overview

Develop a content calendar that includes all the platforms that you plan to post on.

It should reflect the goals you’ve established for each social profile, so that everything you post supports your business goals.

To begin developing a content calendar follow the 80/20 rule:

80 percent of your posts should inform, educate, or entertain your audience

20 percent can directly promote your brand.

You can adjust this ratio and include more branded content if you find that your audience is receptive to brand messages.

Once your calendar is created, you can use scheduling tools to schedule your posts in advance.


## Slide 40

Content Calendar Overview

Use metrics to guide creating your social media content calendar. Start by evaluating which posts had the highest reach, clicks and engagements.

Take those topics and develop a social media content calendar that maps out all the planned posts for the month.

The first six months of a content calendar is all about test and learn. Plan to test the following:

Short post copy vs. long post copy

Stock images vs. real images

Images vs. video

If you’re running ads, test different calls to action.

## Slide 47

Social Media Influencers Overview

Definition: a social media influencer is someone who wields their influence through social media. When you hire an influencer to promote your product or brand, that’s influencer marketing.

Nano-influencer: 10,000 followers or fewer.

Micro-influencer: 10,000 to 100,000 followers.

Macro-influencer: 100,000 to 1 million followers.

Mega-influencer: 1 million+ followers.


## Slide 48

The details:

The number one goal for brands is to reach new target customers.

Most influencer campaigns will require a marketing budget to pay the influencer and then promote the content through paid social media posts.

You can reach out to them directly or through a specialty influencer agency.

When vetting influencers:

Look to see if they fit your brand’s core values.

Determine if they’ve partnered with a competitor in the last 6 months.

See if their sponsored posts get as much engagement as their regular social media content.  Do they have too many paid posts?

Scan for fake followers using the ratio of social media engagements/number of followers. If their engagement rate is below 5-10%, they’ve likely bought fake followers and you won’t see a good ROI on your influencer investment.


## Slide 49

The Federal Trade Commission Rules

Influencers must follow the guidelines of being an influencer by disclosing it’s a paid post through the usage of something such as #ad or #sponsored that is shown up front in the post.

Video reviews must include both written and verbal disclosure of the partnership. It must be within the video itself (not just the description).

The influencer and the brand could be fined if these rules aren’t followed.

Specific rules vary by country but anyone viewing the post must understand it’s a paid promotion.

## Slide 51

The 3 Rs

Influence is made up of relevance, reach and resonance.

Relevance: Do their followers align with your target audience and do they seem like a credible person to talk about your industry?

Reach: The number of people you could potentially connect with through an influencer’s followers. A smaller following can be just as effective (and cheaper) as a larger following if their followers are highly engaged.

Resonance: the potential level of engagement the influencer can create with an audience relevant to your brand.


## Slide 52

Best practices

Let them guide the content creation process. They know what will and won’t work for their followers. Overmanaged content usually doesn’t perform as well.

Provide them with guidelines for the content they create to ensure it meets your brand standards.

Ensure they mention your brand prominently and upfront in the content they create. Not everyone reads or watches content until the end.

Measure your work through not just likes and shares but also by giving each influencer a specific UTM link or their own discount code to track their impact. You can also ask them to share detailed campaign metrics with you as part of your upfront contract.
