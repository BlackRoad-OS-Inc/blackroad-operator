1. What 40% of your happiness is determined by?

About 40% of your happiness comes from what you do, your actions, mindset, and habits. It’s how you handle challenges, set goals, and take care of your mental and physical health. So, a lot of it is in your hands if you’re willing to put in the effort.

2. What are the best ways to release dopamine and increase your happiness?

To boost happiness, focus on things like exercise, helping others, being creative, or even just hanging out with friends. These things trigger dopamine, which is the brain's feel-good chemical, and can improve your mood.

3. What is flow? Have you ever experienced flow in your life? If so, describe it.

Flow is when you’re so into something that you lose track of time. It could be a hobby, a project, or even studying when you’re fully engaged. When you're in flow, things feel effortless, and you’re just in the zone. Have you ever felt that while doing something you enjoy?

4. What do happy people do when they go through adversity?

Happy people deal with adversity by staying focused on solutions, maintaining an optimistic attitude, and learning from challenges. They don’t ignore problems, but they work through them and often turn tough moments into growth.

5. What did you learn about the difference in happiness between people who earn $50,000 per year and $50,000,000 per year?

Money doesn’t automatically make you happier once your basic needs are covered. People making $50,000/year can have similar happiness levels to those earning $50 million. The real difference comes from things like relationships, personal growth, and having a sense of purpose.

6. How important is family to happiness?

Family can definitely impact happiness, but it’s about the quality of relationships. Having a supportive, loving family helps, but it’s even more important to have those deep connections—whether with family or friends.

7. What three intrinsic goals are important to happiness?

The three things that really contribute to lasting happiness are personal growth, meaningful relationships, and helping others. These intrinsic goals bring fulfillment that goes beyond material success.

8. What is the least happy country in the world? Why?

Countries like Afghanistan are often ranked among the least happy. Factors like war, poverty, and instability make life really difficult for people, which affects their overall well-being.

9. What is Karoshi?

Karoshi is a Japanese term for death by overwork. It refers to the extreme work culture where people push themselves too hard, often at the cost of their health and happiness. It’s a reminder that balancing work and life is essential for long-term well-being.

10. What can we learn about work/life balance from Japan?

Japan has struggled with overwork for a long time, but they’re now starting to focus on better work-life balance. It’s a reminder that rest and personal time are just as important as being productive at work.

11. What is Gross National Happiness in Bhutan?

In Bhutan, they measure happiness through Gross National Happiness (GNH), which looks at things like mental health, community well-being, and environmental sustainability. It’s a broader way of measuring success, beyond just economic factors.

12. What are co-housing communities in Denmark?

Co-housing communities in Denmark are shared living spaces where people live independently but also share common areas, like kitchens and gardens. These spaces help create strong social bonds and a sense of belonging, which boosts overall happiness.

13. How does being spiritual or religious make some people more unhappy?

For some people, religion or spirituality can bring pressure or guilt, especially if they feel like they’re not living up to certain expectations. But for others, it can give them purpose and a sense of belonging, which can make them happier.

14. Why do people in Okinawa, Japan live longer than everyone else?

People in Okinawa live longer because they focus on staying active, eating a healthy diet, and maintaining strong social connections. Their lifestyle helps them stay healthy, both physically and mentally, for much longer.

15. What does icharibachode mean?

Icharibachode is an Okinawan saying that means “treat every encounter as if it’s the last.” It’s about living in the moment and really valuing your relationships with others.

16. What does "monchu" mean?

Monchu refers to a sense of responsibility for your community and family. It’s about supporting each other and being there for the people around you, which creates stronger, happier connections.

17. What do we learn about the people in Namibia that makes them happy?

In Namibia, the Herero people show us that happiness comes from community. They prioritize helping each other, working together, and building close relationships, which contributes a lot to their happiness.

18. How does compassion (acts of kindness) relate to a person's happiness?

Being kind and compassionate activates areas of the brain that make you feel good. It creates a positive feedback loop, so when you help others, it makes you feel happier too.

19. Happiness is a skill. How could you practice or grow your happiness each day?

You can grow your happiness by practicing things like gratitude, mindfulness, setting small goals, and spending time with people who bring you joy. Just making those small, intentional habits can really shift your mindset.

20. What's one item from the documentary today you've learned that can help you be more happy in the future?

One thing that stood out to me was the power of gratitude. Practicing it regularly—like writing down things you’re grateful for—can shift your perspective and make you feel happier in the long run.
