## Slide 20

MINERAL RIGHTS
Subsurface
May be transferred when sold or kept by seller
*often held by a third party (corporation/government)

## Slide 21

VERTICAL LEASE
Property owner leases mineral, surface, and air rights to different tenants

## Slide 22

IMPROVEMENTS
Attached to the land
Permanent
Ex. house, fence, road, landscaping
*cannot be removed without demolition

## Slide 23

DEED
Transfers real estate/property
Purpose is voluntary transfer of title between owner/grantor/ seller and receiver/grantee/ buyer
*a seller of real property is always required to provide written deed
*do not prove or guarantee ownership
*do not need to be recorded- TITLE passses upon acceptance of the GRANTEE

## Slide 24

BILL OF SALE
Transfers personal property

## Slide 25

FIXTURES
Personal property that was attached to an improvement
Automatically transferred by the deed
*also called APPURTENANCES

## Slide 26

ATTACHMENT
Fixture is permanently attached
Ex. built-in

## Slide 27

ADAPTATION
Fixture is unique to the property
Ex. keys

## Slide 28

AGREEMENT
Fixture is agreed to be kept in/excluded from the purchase contract
*written contract is final decision on what stays with the property or goes with the buyer

## Slide 29

EXCEPTIONS TO FIXTURES
Trade fixtures
Emblements

## Slide 30

TRADE FIXTURES
Tenant-installed additions that are part of their trade or business
Ex. ovens for a restaurant
Tenant can take trade fixtures before the lease terminates
*if tenant does not remove trade fixtures they become the landlord/lessor’s property

## Slide 31

PERSONAL PROPERTY
Anything that belongs to one person and can be easily taken with them
*must be included in writing (BILL OF SALE)

## Slide 32

EMBLEMENTS
Annual crops belong to whoever planted them
*this will never be the right answer according to the one bitch

## Slide 33

METHODS OF LAND DESCRIPTION
Metes and bounds
Rectangular (government) survey
Plat

## Slide 34

LEGAL DESCRIPTIONS
Describe the land, not any improvements
*address is not a legal description because it can change

## Slide 35

SURVEY
Used to create/confirm a legal description
Uses monuments
Measures property lines and location of houses, easements, and encroachments

## Slide 36

METES AND BOUNDS
Begins and ends at point of beginning (POB)
Measures in feet and compass degrees
*always measured in the clockwise direction

## Slide 37

RECTANGULAR SURVEY
Locates parcel of land in a grid system
Each “township” has 36 sections
Sections are 1x1 mile squares
*range goes North South
*tier goes East West
*sections contain 640 acres

## Slide 38

FEET IN 1 ACRE
43,560

## Slide 39

PLAT (RECORDED PLAT)
Map created by a survey for subdivisions or neighborhoods
*most common in urban residential areas
*also called LOT, BLOCK, SUBDIVISION SYSTEM

## Slide 40

LAND USE CLASSIFICATIONS
Residential
Commercial
Industrial/ manufacturing
Agricultural
Mixed

## Slide 41

BUFFER ZONE
Area between two different land use zones
*often parks or open spaces

## Slide 42

LAND USE RESTRICTIONS
Purpose is to protect against uncontrolled growth
New constructions require building permits
*state sets minimum construction safety requirements
*city/county codes can be stricter than state codes
*strictest rules are used

## Slide 43

CERTIFICATE OF OCCUPANCY
Obtained after fulfilling all building permit requirements

## Slide 44

ZONING LAWS
Determine land use classifications
Set at a local (city/county) level
*changed through amendments

## Slide 45

NONCONFORMING USE
Type of grandfather clause
Allows owner to continue their current use if zoning is amended and no longer allows that use
*owner may not enlarge or rebuild if destroyed
*any new owner may be required to meet the new zoning requirements

## Slide 46

VARIANCE
Allows individuals to have an exception to zoning to prevent economic hardship
Ex. house built past buildable footprint

## Slide 47

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT
Individual property can be used for a special purpose in the public’s best interest
Ex. church or daycare in a residential zone
*also called SPECIAL USE permit

## Slide 48

POLICE POWER
Public control of land
Enact and enforce laws governing land use
Planning zoning and building codes

## Slide 49

EMINENT DOMAIN
Right to take land for public use
Owner receives compensation plus damages ($$$)
*process is called CONDEMNATION
*example of INVOLUNTARY ALIENATION

## Slide 50

CONDEMNATION
Process by which the government takes private land for public use
Ex. buying someone’s house to build a highway on top of where it was

## Slide 51

TAXATION
Right the government has to collect property taxes
Right the government has to charge a property that benefits from a public improvement special assessments 
*property taxes (aka government) have priority over all other LIENS

## Slide 52

SPECIAL ASSESSMENTS
Charges against properties that benefit from public improvements
Ex. “tax/fee” for street lights and sidewalks
*often charged on a $/frontage square foot basis
*can create a LIEN if not paid

## Slide 53

PROPERTY TAXES
Money paid to the government on property annually
Paid in the county where the property is located
Enforced by a foreclosable lien
*calculated AD VALOREM
*CIRCLE FORMULA

## Slide 54

AD VALOREM
Means assessed value
Assessed value x tax rate = annual property tax
*CIRCLE FORMULA

## Slide 55

ESCHEAT
Government’s right to take property that has been abandoned
Owner dies with no will (intestate) and without heirs

## Slide 56

CIRCLE FORMULA

## Slide 57

VOLUNTARY ALIENATION
Being asked or choosing to surrender/sell property

## Slide 58

INVOLUNTARY ALIENATION
Being forced to surrender property
Adverse possession
Easement by prescription/ prescriptive easement
*granted through a court procedure

## Slide 59

ESTATE
The amount and kind of legal right (legal interest) in real property that allows possession

## Slide 60

TYPES OF ESTATES
Freehold estates
Leasehold estates

## Slide 61

FREEHOLD ESTATES
Fee simple absolute
Fee simple defeasible (defeated)
Life estate
*ownership has indefinite duration (can go on forever)

## Slide 62

FEE SIMPLE ABSOLUTE
Complete bundle of rights
Maximum control of property
Lasts forever
*TRANSFERABLE and inheritable

## Slide 63

FEE SIMPLE DEFEASIBLE
Created by deed condition
Usually a gift
If deed condition is violated, may revert to grantor
*TRANSFERABLE and inheritable

## Slide 64

GRANTOR
A person giving something to someone (deed)
Ex. seller of a property gives the deed

## Slide 65

GRANTEE
A person receiving something from someone (deed)
Ex. buyer of a property receives the deed

## Slide 66

LIFE ESTATE
Tenant (grantee) is the owner
Measured by the length of someone’s life
Upon the death of whoever was decided, estate returns to grantor or third party
Tenant may sell or lease the property, but property still returns to grantor at death of whoever was measured
*grantor is then called the REVERTER
*or third party is then called REMAINDERMAN
*only TRANSFERABLE not inheritable

## Slide 67

LENDOR
A person who loans something to someone else (loan)
Ex. bank

## Slide 68

LENDEE
A person who is loaned something (loan)
Ex. home buyer

## Slide 69

LESSOR
A person who rents something to someone (apartment)
Is paid rent
Ex. landlord

## Slide 70

LESSEE
A person who rents something from someone
Pays rent
Ex. us at the Hub
*also called TENANT

## Slide 71

LEASEHOLD ESTATES
Estate for years
Periodic estate
Estate at will
Estate at sufferance
*possession is for a fixed term (definite duration)
*landlord/LESSOR holds a LEASED FEE ESTATE
*LESSEE ownership is subject to the lease agreement

## Slide 72

ESTATE FOR YEARS
Specific termination date (end date)
No death terminates
Sale of property does not terminate
*can also be called TENANCY FOR YEARS

## Slide 73

PERIODIC ESTATE
Renews automatically
Ex. month to month
Terminated by advance notice by either party
*can also be called PERIODIC TENANCY

## Slide 74

ESTATE AT WILL
Indefinite duration
tenant occupies at landlord’s discretion
“No fixed term”
Terminated by death, sale, or notice by either party
*can also be called TENANCY AT WILL

## Slide 75

ESTATE AT SUFFERANCE
When a tenant stays past the termination of the lease
Owner may convict tenant or accept rent
*can also be called TENANCY AT SUFFERANCE
*tenant HOLDOVER
*if owner accepts rent it becomes a PERIODIC ESTATE

## Slide 76

ENCUMBRANCES
Any claim, right, or interest held by a party that is not a legal owner of the property
Easements
Liens
Deed restrictions
Encroachments 
*attach to and bind real property (continue after sold)
*may impact the ability of the seller to transfer TITLE

## Slide 77

TYPES OF EASEMENTS
Appurtenant easement
Easement in gross
Easement by necessity

## Slide 78

APPURTENANT EASEMENT
Has a dominant holder who receives the use (the easement) and a servient holder who gives use (the easement)
Ex. driveway across a neighbor’s land
*transfers with the land

## Slide 79

DOMINANT HOLDER
A person receiving and easement (use of another’s land)
