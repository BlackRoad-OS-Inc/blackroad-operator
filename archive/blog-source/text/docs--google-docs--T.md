Details

Ed Mills, a policy analyst at Raymond James, told CBS MoneyWatch that Trump’s tax, trade, tariff, and immigration plans could greatly affect the economy and cause a rise in inflation again.

What could happen with inflation?

Many consumers still worry about inflation, especially after dealing with high prices during the pandemic. Although the U.S. inflation rate is close to the Federal Reserve’s target of 2% per year, many Americans still feel prices are high because they are rising more slowly rather than going down.

Economists warn that Trump’s plans could trigger inflation again. Tariffs act like sales taxes that American consumers pay, not the countries exporting to the U.S. Additionally, Trump's idea to deport millions of immigrants could increase inflation. Without enough workers, employers may have to pay higher wages.

Jacob Channel, chief economist at LendingTree, noted that Trump's main proposals, tariffs and mass deportations, would likely raise prices because they would make it harder for businesses to produce goods. The proposed 10% tariff on all imports and 60% tariffs on Chinese goods could cost a typical middle-class household an extra $1,700 a year, according to the Peterson Institute for International Economics. Trump’s plans might raise the inflation rate by as much as 1 percentage point, pushing it to about 3.4%, above the Fed’s target of 2%, according to Andrzej Skiba of RBC Global Asset Management.

Could the economy grow faster?

The economy may see slight growth with Trump's corporate tax cuts, but this effect might drop over time, especially due to the impact of deporting millions of immigrants. Ryan Sweet from Oxford Economics said real GDP growth could be 0.3 percentage points higher in 2026 compared to continuing current policies. However, he added that GDP growth might eventually decline by 0.6 percentage points in 2028 due to deportations and higher tariffs.

Will housing become more affordable?

It seems unlikely, according to Lisa Sturtevant, chief economist at Bright MLS.

First, if Trump’s plans cause inflation again, the Federal Reserve might not lower interest rates further. Higher mortgage rates would remain if borrowing costs don’t decrease. Second, deporting millions of undocumented immigrants could harm the housing market, which already lacks enough homes. This sector relies on these workers to build new houses. Sturtevant said Trump’s deportation proposal could hurt the construction industry by reducing the labor force and slowing down new housing projects. Additionally, tariffs would raise building costs.

The day after the election, financial markets showed concern about future inflation, as 10-year U.S. Treasury yields rose to their highest since July.

Trump's proposed tariffs on imports, especially from China, are expected to push prices up for consumers. Treasury yields also affect mortgage rates. As yields increased, mortgage rates rose in recent weeks with traders betting on a Trump victory. While the S&P 500 stock index surged, there are risks to the economy with his policies.

How tariffs could push up inflation

Trump has suggested up to a 60% tariff on Chinese imports and up to 20% on imports from other countries. Economists believe these tariffs would make merchants raise prices for consumers.

If Trump enacts a low-end agenda with just a 10% tariff, core inflation could increase to 4% next year from the current 2.7%, according to economists Jay H. Bryson and Michael Pugliese at Wells Fargo Securities.

Higher inflation would lead to more expensive loans. If Trump’s tariffs raise inflation, the Federal Reserve might keep interest rates higher than they would otherwise. This situation would increase interest rates on various loans. The Fed reduced the fed funds rate recently, but rates may not drop further.
