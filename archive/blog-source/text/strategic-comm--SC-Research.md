## Slide 7

Primary - Qualitative Research

Five qualitative research techniques

Content analysis – this can be done in a quantitative way as well, but is often qualitative.

Interviews – long-form discussions one-to-one.

Focus groups –  approx.15 people around the table with a facilitator.

Copy testing – asking what they think of the advertisements.

Ethnographic techniques – In a true ethnography, the researcher “lives among” the participants and observes their day-to-day practices

NO NUMBERS so it is qualitative. Deeper, richer responses.


## Slide 8

Primary - Quantitative Research

Polls, surveys, experiments conducted using precise, scientific sampling methods

Key elements

Random sampling – you select a random sample of a larger population at a size that allows you to make conclusions about the population in general – I want to know what UMN undergraduates think about vaping on campus, so I randomly select 1000 of them to complete a survey (randomly select means that every student has an equal possibility of being selected). We assume that the 1000 students who respond are representative of the larger population. THIS IS DONE WITH SURVEYS

Random assignment – You randomly select individuals to go into either condition A or condition B of an experiment. Therefore, everybody has an equal opportunity of being selected for group A or group B. We assume that any differences between group A and group B are due to the items we manipulated in the experiment vs. characteristics of group A or group B.

Sample size – must be large enough to make assumptions that the data is normally distributed (>30) and that it is reflective of the population in the case of random sampling.
