Venus Fly Traps

By: Alexa Amundson - Venus Fly Trap Mother to Chomp

Obtaining Them:

Q: Where can I go to save a Venus fly trap?

You can buy your death box at Bachmann’s if they have them in stock

Q: How should I get my Venus fly trap out of his death box?

Cut the plastic box vertically and then gently unfold the box sideways so the plant doesn’t get disturbed/set off the traps and exude energy.

Animal Safety:

Q: Are Venus fly traps safe for pets?

Non-Toxic to Dogs, Non-Toxic to Cats, Non-Toxic to Horses

Feeding:

Q: What do they eat

They prefer alive spiders and small bugs.

Q: How big of a bug is okay for me to feed them?

As long as they can completely close their trap regarding the bug’s size, it should be okay.

Q: How often should I feed them indoors?

Once every 1-2 weeks

Q: How often should I feed them outdoors?

They’ll eat when they want, so you don’t have to feed them bugs

Q: How often should I feed them if they live indoors and outdoors?

Bring them outside and let them sunbathe for a bit. Check and see if a new trap is closed. Then check if you can see a bug in the closed trap by holding it up to the light.

I like to do this every 3 days because I’m scared of bugs and don’t want to overfeed the Venus fly trap

Q: what if my Venus fly trap opens his trap and there is still a bug inside?

Gently remove the bug, and do not try to feed it to them again

Watering/Drinking:

Q: What type of watering do they require?

They are a bottom-watering plant! This means that you don’t necessarily water them. They drink from a bottom basin and suck the water up!

Q: How can you set up a bottom watering pot?

We used the original pot they came in, and we got a normal pot that would fit the original pot inside of it!

Next, we filled the bottom of the new pot with river pebbles

Then, we filled the water with ¼-½ inch of water, not including the rock depth.

We could tell how much water was there by checking the damp water line when putting the old pot in the new pot. Again, this should be a ¼-½ depth.

Q: What kind of water can they drink?

Reverse osmosis water

Rainwater

Distilled water (I got it at quik trip)

Q: How do I know if they’re getting enough water?

The soil should be damp, but your finger should not be wet if you touch the soil.

Just touch the soil, and if your finger is wet, don’t water it for a while until it’s just damp.

Q: Do I need to provide water for my Venus fly trap in the winter?

Yes, but do not feed them

Soil:

Q: What kind of soil does my Venus fly trap need?

If you are mixing the soil yourself, a 1:1 mixture of unenriched peat moss and perlite is ideal

Or a standard carnivorous plant soil mixture

Repotting:

Q: How often should you repot your Venus fly trap?

After saving him from his death box

Once a year after that

Q: How do I repot a Venus fly trap?

Remove the Venus fly trap from the pot carefully to avoid setting off a trap

Expose the roots

With your fingers, gently break away the old potting medium from the roots of the Venus flytrap. If necessary, separate multiple plants from one another to be re-planted separately.

Repot the plant with the new soil mixture by making a hole with your thumb in the new soil mixture

Dormancy/Hibernation:

Q: What is dormancy?

Venus fly traps must go through dormancy to survive.

Their leaves will blacken and fall off, making the plant appear dead. It is not dead but merely resting.

Place your Venus Flytrap in a cool place, around 40F (4C), with little light in the winter months. The dormancy period needs to be around three months.

Flytraps require 3-4 months of winter dormancy triggered by cold temperatures (below 50°F or 10°C) and shorter daylight hours. Before entering dormancy, they will drop their upright leaves and stop growing altogether. While dormant, your plants can withstand overnight frosts down to 20°F (-7°C).

Q: Do I need to provide water for my Venus fly trap in the winter?

Yes, but do not feed them

Ideal Coloration:

Q: What color should my Venus fly trap be?

Green with pink means they’re very healthy. In most cases

Q: Why does mine have black traps?

Black traps should be cut so the plant doesn’t waste energy trying to help a trap that’s dead

Traps and Death:

Q: Can I put my fingers in it?

Please don’t put your fingers in it; it uses a ton of energy to close its trap

Q: Will the traps die?

Each trap can close approx 3-5 times then it will die. It’s okay, though.

Q: Will the traps grow back?

Each trap is like a limb and will grow back. Once it is entirely black, cut the trap off so the Venus fly trap can make a stronger and bigger trap.

Length of Time for a Trap to Stay Closed:

Q: how long will a trap stay closed?

The first time you disturb a trap, it will stay shut for 7-13 hours

If you disturb it again shortly after, it will be 5-7 days.

It does this to protect itself and maximize energy expenditure

Please remember it can only close a trap 3-5 times, so DO NOT SHOVE YOUR FINGERS IN IT

Understanding Plant Happiness:

Q: how do I know if they’re sad?

Your Venus flytrap is losing its colors

The petioles are growing wide

Venus fly traps are not producing traps

The plant is droopy and wilting

The traps have lost all red coloring

The traps stop working; they won’t close

The plant is losing leaves and reducing in size

Q: How do I know if a Venus fly trap is happy?

Strong and vibrant roots

Tight and firm traps

White flower blooms

The inside of the trap will also be vibrant in color, with either red, pink, or green. If your plant is not getting enough sunlight, its leaves will start to fade, and the inside of the trap may turn black

Flowering:

Q: How does the Venus fly trap attract insects?

The traps smell like fruit and will attract flies and other insects!

When healthy, they will also make a flower!! The flower allows bees to pollinate the plant without getting tricked into eating the fruit smell!

Q: What to do when a flower is blooming?

Cut it once it's reached about 5 cm tall.

Flowering can be exhausting for Venus Flytraps, and most plants will grow more vigorously during summer if prevented from flowering.

Q: How do I ensure my Venus fly trap stays healthy while flowering?

Water Your Venus Flytrap with Distilled Water.

Ditch the Humidity Dome.

Plenty of Sunshine.

Feed Your Venus Flytrap (but not too much)

Respect Your Flytrap's Dormancy.

Weather:

Q: What kind of weather can my Venus fly trap be in?

Humidity -- The Venus Fly Trap doesn't require extremely high humidity, but above 50%.

Temperature -- Can range from 70° - 95° F (21° - 35° C) and down to 40° F (5° C) in the winter
