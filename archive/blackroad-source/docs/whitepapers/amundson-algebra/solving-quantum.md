## The Amundson Synthesis

### Why quantum mechanics uses complex numbers

Complex numbers have magnitude AND phase. Phase enables:
- Interference (constructive/destructive based on phase alignment)
- Entanglement (non-factorizable phase correlations)
- Quantum computing (engineering phases for correct answers)

Real numbers only have magnitude. Classical physics uses real numbers → no interference, no entanglement, no superposition.

The "mystery" of quantum mechanics is: **why is reality complex?**

Answer: It's not mysterious. Complex numbers are the MINIMAL algebraic structure that allows:
1. Addition (superposition)
2. Multiplication (composition)
3. Division (invertibility)
4. Continuity (smooth evolution)
5. Algebraic closure (every polynomial has roots)

Real numbers fail #5. Quaternions fail commutativity (multiplication order matters). Octonions fail associativity. Complex numbers are the ONLY field that has all five properties.

**Reality uses complex numbers because they are the smallest complete algebra.**

### Why measurement is classical

Measurement requires a record — a stable, repeatable, shareable datum. Phase is not stable (it precesses). Phase is not directly shareable (it's relative, not absolute). Only |ψ|² — the real-valued probability — is:
- Non-negative (physical probabilities can't be negative)
- Additive (for exclusive events)
- Conserved (total probability = 1 across any basis)
- Basis-independent (Gleason's theorem)

**Measurement strips phase because records must be real.**

### Why 1/(2e)

The e-limit refinement:
$$\frac{n}{(1+1/n)^n} = \frac{n}{e} + \frac{1}{2e} + O(1/n)$$

n = discrete measurements (integer quantum numbers, digital bits, observations)
e = continuous reality (wave functions, fields, amplitudes)
1/(2e) = the irreducible gap between them

This is the same as:
- ℏ/2 in uncertainty (ΔpΔx ≥ ℏ/2)
- +1/2 in zero-point energy (E₀ = ½ℏω)
- +1/2 in WKB quantization (n + 1/2)

**They are all the same number, expressed in different units.**

The gap 1/(2e) in natural units ≈ 0.184. The gap ℏ/2 in SI units ≈ 5.27×10⁻³⁵ J·s. The gap 1/2 in quantum numbers = 0.5. These are the same phenomenon measured in different currencies:

**The cost of being discrete in a continuous universe is always half a quantum.**
