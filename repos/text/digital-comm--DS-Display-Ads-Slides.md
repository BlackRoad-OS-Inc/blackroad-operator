## Slide 8

Display advertising

Definition: a method of attracting the audience of a website, social media platform or other digital mediums to take a specific action. Often made up of text-based, image or video advertisements that encourage the user to click-through to a landing page and act (e.g. make a purchase).

Most campaigns are charged on a cost-per-click basis.

While we still have website cookies, they are can be an effective way to retarget users who have already visited your website but did not take an additional action or complete a started action. Display advertising can follow you from website to website, app to app.


## Slide 9

Types of display advertising

Banner ads: can appear anywhere on a website: top, sidebar or in between article sections

Interstitial ads: Appear as web pages that are served to users before they are directed to the original page they requested. Like unskippable YouTube videos, they’re annoying but guarantee reach and impressions.


## Slide 10

Types of display advertising

Rich media: include interactive elements, such as video, audio and clickable elements. Can be effective for driving lead gens if your targeting is spot on.

Video ads: As discussed in our social media advertising lecture, many consumer prefer videos to text when consuming content.


## Slide 11

Pros and Cons

Pros:

Ad type is easily adaptable to your goals/needs

Through Google Display Network, you’re guaranteed extensive reach and the ability to target very specific groups of website visitors

Measurable: Google Ads and Google Analytics offer easy measurement and optimization.

Cons:

Banner blindness due to the volume of banners deployed on websites

Ad blocker technology makes it harder to reach your intended audience.

The average CTR for display ads is 0.06% so a good digital strategy will have a complimentary strategy such as SEO/SEM.

## Slide 13

Creative best practices

Use the right language by having multiple display ad variations for your various target audiences (for example luxury, budget, business and weekend travelers of a hotel chain)

Establish urgency in your copy, make it bold and short so it’s easy to absorb

Use clutter-free imagery or slight animation to attract the eye

Have a single, clear and compelling call-to-action

Develop a customized landing page that directly connects back to the call-to-action aka deal that your display ad is promoting.


## Slide 14

Display ad measurement

Impressions: how many times your ad is seen on a website

Reach: how many unique people saw your ad. Optimize your ad frequency so you’re continuously reaching new people.

CTR (click-through rate): the percentage of people who click through to your landing page, if this is low you should change your targeting or creative

Conversion rate: the most important KPI, the number of people who click to the landing page and take the desired action from your call-to-action. A low conversion rate means your target audience is incorrect or your landing page isn’t delivering the content people expect.


## Slide 15

Remarketing and the cookie problem

Chrome will be going cookie free in 2023, Mozilla Firefox blocked them in 2019 and Apple’s Safari in 2020.

The removal of third-party cookies from Chrome means Google will stop selling ads targeted to people’s individual browsing behaviors and Chrome will no longer allow cookies that collect data.

Without third-party cookies, expectations around how online marketing campaigns are targeted, tracked and measured will need to change completely and marketers will need to find new ways to attribute conversions, frequency cap ad placements and retarget site visitors outside of the Google ecosystem.

However, Google is not changing any policies around how businesses collect or use data gathered directly from Google-owned products such as Ads and Analytics, so you will still be able to remarket to people using Google’s first-party data within its own tools.

Google has repeatedly pushed back their cookieless date so it remains to be seen if they’re actually implement this drastic change.


## Slide 16

Native Ads

Definition:  the practice of blending a paid ad with the intended platform’s look, feel and function

According to MediaRadar, native ads generate an 8.8x increase in clicks compared to display ads.

Whether on social media or a media publisher’s website, brands are posting on a website where people are already visiting and aiming to offer informative, educational, entertaining content that increases brand awareness.
