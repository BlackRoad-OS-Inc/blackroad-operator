HOW A CONTRACT IS CREATED

a contract is a voluntary, binding agreement between two      or more competent parties to do (or not do) a specific thing.

Implied Contract

Actions indicate intent

No implied contracts in transfer of any interest in real estate

Express (declared) Contract

Can be oral or written

To be enforceable, the majority of real estate must be “express written documents”.

Bilateral

Promise exchanged for a promise

Both parties are bound to perform

Example: purchase contract— “I promise to sell and you promise to buy”

Unilateral

Promise exchanged for performance

Only one party is bound

Example: option contract— “I promise to sell if you decide to buy”

CLASSIFICATION OF CONTRACTS

Valid

Meets all legal requirements; contains all essential elements

Binding and enforceable in court by both parties

Void

No legal effect ; not enforceable by either party

Lacks one or more of the essential elements of a valid contract

Example: an oral contract for the purchase of real estate

Voidable

A voidable contract appears to be valid, but one party may disaffiliated it because the party is a minor or was subject to duress, fraud, or misrepresentation.

The disadvantaged party may take legal action to disaffirm the contract.

Letter of Intent

It has no legal obligation, but is a way for the buyer to seek information. About a potential transaction.

It is most often used in commercial sales.

It does not have to be tied to one project.

The negotiation period, no contract exists yet.

In the offer stage, earnest money is not usually deposited because there is not a contract yet.

Any strike-through changes on an offer must be initialed (and dated for clarity) by all parties to be enforceable.

Upon a meeting of the minds and communication of acceptance by the parties, a contract is formed.

Termination of the offer prior to acceptance can occur through any of the following:

STATUTE OF FRAUDS AND STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS

Statute of Frauds

The statute of frauds requires that, in order to be enforceable, certain contracts must be in writing and signed by all parties that are expected to perform.

The statute-of-frauds requirement includes all contracts for the transfer of interests in real estate

An exception is a lease for 12 months or less

The purpose Is to prevent problems associated with oral contracts.

Real estate professionals are obligated to put all agreements of the parties in writing so that the parties have recourse if there is a breach

Statute of limitations

The statute of limitations sets the amount of time parties will be given to file a claim or lawsuit.

APPRAISAL

Definition of an Appraisal

An estimate or opinion of market value supported by an analysis of relevant property data

Appraisals are regulated at both the state and federal level

Must be done by licenses appraiser

More accurate than a CMA or a broker’s price opinion (BPO)

The Uniform Residential Appraisal Report (URAR) is the standard form typically used for residential appraisals.

Appraisal Licensing

Appraisers must be licensed or certified according to state law, and in accordance with federal regulations.

Appraisers may have further designations, such as MAI (Member of the Appraisal Institute) or SRA (Senior Residential Appraiser), from the Appraisal Institute.

Appraisers are also expected to follow the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP).

Purpose and Function of Appraisals

Most appraisals seem to estimate market value on behalf of a lender but could also be used by sellers estimating the market value of their property.

An appraisal is required for all federally related loans, such as conventional, FHA, and VA loans from regulated lenders, it is not required for seller-carry loans.

An appraisal assures that the relationship between the amount of the loan and the value of the property is appropriate (loan-to-value ratio).

Appraisals are also used for insurance, condemnation proceedings, and if a borrower wants to stop paying private mortgage insurance (PMI).

CMA/BPO/broker opinion of value (BOV)

a real estate licensee’s job is to perform a competitive market analysis, not an appraisal.

The licensee researches and evaluates comparable properties that have recently sold.

It is similar to the sales comparison approach (will be covered later).

A CMA/BPO/BOV assists a seller and a buyer in determining value

A broker may charge a fee for the CMA/BPO/BOV, but should make it clear that it is not an appraisal.

PRINCIPLES OF VALUE

Highest and Best Use

The highest and best use is the use that produces the best return on investment over time.

It typically is the current use, but it could be different.

The highest and best use of a property can change over time, but there can only be one highest and best use at a time.

The appraiser must show current highest and best use in the appraisal.

It would not be be included in a CMA/BPO/BOV.

Anticipation

Future benefits determine the current value

Real estate values are driven by the anticipation of future benefits.

Supply and Demand

Seller’s market versus buyer’s market

Supply: number of available properties (of a certain type in a certain area)

Price moves opposite of supply ; when supply goes up, price goes down

Demand: number of properties that will be purchased

Price moves with demand ; when demand goes up ; price goes up

Determines sales price and rental rates

Substitution

Substitution is the most important principle in appraisal.

It is the basis of the sales comparison approach and is used in all three approaches

In real estate (like any commodity) , the lowest price with the highest value will sell first.

The buyer considers the cost of acquiring an equally desirable substitute that meets the same criteria of time, function, and utility.

To be accurate, all approaches to value must consider what the market will pay.

Conformity

Values tend to move toward surroundings

Value is impacted what properties are different sizes or have different amenities in the same area.

Regression is when the value of over improved property declines

Progression is when the value of under improved property increases.

Values are most stable when all properties are similar.

Example: a large house in a neighborhood with small houses will not have as high a value as if it were located near other large properties.

Contribution

Contribution determines the increasing or decreasing returns on improvements.

The value of an improvement is not what it costs to build but what it adds to the market value of the property.

Increasing returns are improvements that add more value than they cost

Decreasing returns are improvements that add less value than they cost.

Value is determined by what the market will pay, not by the price of an improvement or the cost of installation

Example: a swimming pool that costs $5,000 to purchase and install might add $10,000 in value in California, but in Minnesota, it might decrease value of a property by $10,000.

METHODS OF VALUATION

Sales comparison / market data approach

key points

It is the most important method for residential property and vacant land

The estimate of value is based on adjusted sale prices of similar properties that were recently sold.

It is similar to what brokers use to determine value when doing a CMA.

Area of improvements (square feet)

Square feet is typically based on exterior dimensions of the finished, heated living area.

The finished areas above and below grade have different values.

Square footage is often determined by local, state, or multiple listing service (MLS) guidelines.

Subject property

Property being appraised

Never adjust the subject

Comparable sales (comps)

Comparables are similar properties that were recently sold.

Adjustments are always made to the sold comparables to make them similar to the subject.

The comparables with the least adjustments generally give a stronger indication of value.

In a rapidly changing market, comparables that have sold within 6 months and no more than 12 months should be used.

Primary elements of comparison and adjustment

Lot size

Date of sale (values may be appreciating or depreciating)

Market cycles often create the need for a “time of sale” adjustment

Days on the market

Amenities

Exterior dimensions of improvements (square footage)

Not the purchase price or acquisition cost of subject property

Adjustments are made to the comps

Adjust comparable to make it similar to the subject

If comparable is better than subject, subtract

Example: the subject property has one bathroom and the comparable has two. Subtract the value of the extra bathroom from the comparable’s sale price to make it match the subject property.

If come is worse than subject, add

Value of adjustments may be determined by doing a paired sales analysis

The appraiser finds two properties that are nearly identical, with the exception of the item being adjusted (e.g. a bedroom, fireplace, or other improvement).

Find the value of the bedroom by determining the difference in sale price of the two properties.

Example: determine the value of a bedroom in two nearly identical properties.

Comp 1 has 3 bedrooms and sold for $250,000

Comp 2 has four bedrooms and sold for $254,000

Using the paired sales method, the value of the bedroom is $4,500 ($254,500 - $250,000 = $4,500.)

Cost Approach

Key points

It is the most important method for new construction and unique or special-purpose property, such as churches and schools.

The cost approach is typically used when comparable sales or income is lacking.

Three steps in cost approach

Estimate the cost to build the improvements as if built today, which may be based on reproduction or replacement cost.

Estimate and deduct accrued depreciation in the subject property

Add the value of the land

Land does not depreciate so it’s value must be deducted from the improvements that depreciate.

It is usually evaluated using the market data approach, and then value is added to find the final value

Formula: cost to build new - accrued depreciation + land value  = estimated value

Income Approach

Key point

The income approach is used for properties that generate income, such as an apartment building, shopping center, or office building.

Investment properties

Real estate investors buy for the income the property produces.

Investors determine what they will pay based on a rate of return or capitalization rate.

The capitalization rate (or cap rate) for a property type in a given area can be determined by using past sales. This allows the appraiser to estimate the present value of a property that has not yet closed.

RECONCILIATION

Key points

The appraiser reconciles (correlates) the three approaches to value (sales comparison / market data, cost, and income).

Each approach is weighed and used to determine a subject’s estimated value. It is not an average

The appraiser considers all three approaches but gives the most weight to the approach that is the most appropriate for the property type.

For a single-family property, the appraiser would give the most weight to the sales comparison approach.

For a unique property, like a school, the appraiser would give the most weight to the cost approach.

For an income-producing property like an office building or shopping center, the appraiser would give the most weight to the income approach.

Title Theory vs. Lien Theory

In title theory states, the borrower (mortgagor) actually gives legal title to the lender (mortgagee) and retains equitable title.

Legal title is returned to the borrower only when the debt is paid in full

In theory, the lender actually owns the property until the debt is paid.

In lien theory states, the borrower retains both legal and equitable title. The lender simply has a lien on the property as security for the mortgage debt.

The mortgage or deed of trust is nothing more than collateral for the loan.

Function of a mortgage or deed of trust is to act as security for the promissory note by pledging the real estate property as collateral for the debt.
