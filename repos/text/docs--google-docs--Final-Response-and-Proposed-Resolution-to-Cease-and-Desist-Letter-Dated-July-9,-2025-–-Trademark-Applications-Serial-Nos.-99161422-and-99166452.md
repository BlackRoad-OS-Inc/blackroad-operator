Dear Ms. Martins,

BlackRoad Inc. will neither withdraw its trademark applications nor cede to demands unsupported by the record. Our “BLACKROAD” mark, filed in unwavering good faith, embodies fintech innovation through AI-native, blockchain-powered advisory—services conceptually and commercially distinct from BlackRock’s traditional asset management. Under the Lanham Act’s §2(d) and §43(c), no likelihood of confusion or dilution arises, as illuminated by the DuPont factors:

Mark dissimilarity (Factor 1): “ROAD” evokes adaptive journeys, divergent in both meaning and phonetic impact from the static solidity of “ROCK.”

Goods/services separation (Factor 2): Our AI-native financial advisory and blockchain-integrated systems serve a digital-first niche, far removed from traditional asset management.

Sophisticated consumer base (Factor 4): Our clients exercise a level of discernment akin to the patrons in Tana v. Dantanna’s, 611 F.3d 767 (11th Cir. 2010), where even phonetically similar restaurant marks were allowed to coexist.

No actual confusion (Factor 6): No evidence has been presented, and multiple marks with the prefix “BLACKRO_” have coexisted peacefully in both Class 036 and adjacent classes.

Just as in Tana v. Dantanna’s, where suffix distinctions and market context dispelled the possibility of confusion, so too here does “BLACKROAD” signify innovation and movement, not the foundational stasis connoted by “BLACKROCK.” Likewise, in Zatarain’s, Inc. v. Oak Grove Smokehouse, Inc., 698 F.2d 786 (5th Cir. 1983), market context and descriptive fair use permitted parallel coexistence of similar marks.

The USPTO record supports our position. Over 65 “BLACKRO_” marks exist, with at least four live “BLACKROCK” registrations unaffiliated with your client (e.g., BLACKROCK MICROSYSTEMS, Reg. No. 3783662). Even in Class 036, BLACKROSE (Reg. No. 86728496) remains registered and unopposed. This raises questions of selective enforcement—especially when our mark diverges more in substance and market than others that remain uncontested. If BlackRoad, a startup with distinct positioning and identity, is uniquely targeted, we reserve the right to explore antitrust implications under Sherman Act §2.
