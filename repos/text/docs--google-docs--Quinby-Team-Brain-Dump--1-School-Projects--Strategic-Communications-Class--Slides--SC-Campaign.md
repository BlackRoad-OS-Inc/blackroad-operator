Strategic Communication Campaign:

You will develop a strategic communication campaign based on what you have learned about the planning process. You will select one real organization (company, not-for-profit, etc.) and develop a strategic communication plan for that organization’s current situation (products/services/issues). By the end of the semester, you will submit your campaign as: a “client presentation” (powerpoint/ slides/etc) and accompanying script. The script should be written in full sentences and it can be part of the powerpoint/slides  (in the notes section, for example).

ORGANIZATIONAL PROFILE: Describe your organization, including background data on the organization/sector.

Focus on describing the organization. Depending on the company you selected, it may not be possible to answer all of these questions, but consider: public vs. private company, ownership/board, annual revenue, current market share, location/s, core business, peripheral business, etc. If you are doing a small, private organization, you will not have access to some of these details, but use what you can write about: location, what does the business look like, describe what you can.

SITUATIONAL ANALYSIS: Describe the current situation your campaign is addressing. This should include a complete SWOT analysis.

In your script -- make sure to have a section on each of the SWOT elements. I have seen two common mistakes in this section in previous years: 1. mixing up strengths and opportunities/weaknesses and threats (remember: strengths and weaknesses are internal to the organization and opportunities and threats are large, macro level factors that may relate to your business; and 2. make sure that your SWOT does NOT have any ideas that you plan to propose for your campaign. This is very important. Even if you have some ideas in mind, do not have any of your strategy or tactics mentioned anywhere in the SWOT.

GOAL: Describe and defend the goal and objectives for your campaign (what do you want to accomplish). Make certain that you have measurable objectives.

Make sure you have at least two goals, and at least one objective for each goal. A goal may have more than one objective. Do not list your goals/objectives -- write them out in full sentences.

BACKGROUND RESEARCH: Provide an analysis of how you might conduct original research to learn more about this situation. For example, would you want quantitative data, qualitative data or both? How would you get that data (surveys, interviews, focus groups, etc). NOTE: You do not need to conduct the research, just describe what you would want to do to learn more.

Propose at least two ideas for research and make sure you include, the method (surveys, focus groups, etc) and whether or not this would be qualitative or quantitative or both. You then want to explain how you would recruit people to participate and an example of a few questions you might ask.

TARGET: Who is your campaign going to target (include demographic information, anything you know about lifestyle)? You must use one of the methods we discussed (VALS; SIMMONS) of defining targets. Why will you target that audience? What are some strengths and weaknesses associated with trying to reach this audience?

You need to provide a detailed explanation of your target/s using at least TWO of the segmentation strategies we discussed (ie. demographic, lifestyle, etc). Within these selected segmentation strategies, you will want to provide detail from as many elements as you can (for example: within demographic, include age, gender, income, etc.). Please use either VALS or SIMMONS to provide some detail on your target and flesh it out as best you an. Describe why you will target the audience and the strengths and weaknesses associated with trying to reach this audience.

CAMPAIGN: Describe your strategic communication campaign. You may focus on advertising, public relations or both. You may use traditional media, new media or both. In this section, you need to include your overall strategy (common theme – should have a tagline or slogan that pulls it together), your STP approach, and describe your entire campaign (describe between 3-5 elements). Do not worry about the budget, but your choices should be practical for your topic.

For this section, make sure to first give your STP approach. You should explain each one in a section. Then, you will want to have a section describing your theme/tagline/slogan and explain it thoroughly, connecting it to the STP approach. This central theme should feel integrated within the campaign itself – should connect to the SWOT and goals and audience you have already described. Next, you will describe 3-5 campaign elements. These are tactics you plan to use and how your campaign theme would be embodied through these elements. You will want to be as detailed as possible so that the ideas are clear.

EXECUTION: Execute one piece of your campaign. For example, you may decide to draft one press release, design one magazine advertisement, etc. This is not a design class, and so what I am looking for here is how you have realized your strategic plan through executing one piece.

Please execute one of the items described above and show it as part of the presentation. If you want to do a TV commercial, you do not have to film it, you may storyboard it (your choice). Keep the budget zero for this project. I am fine with you using artwork from a service with the watermarks (123rf.com, etc) to avoid costs. The idea here is to show the concept as best you can. Remember if you decide to execute a social media campaign, you will need to do more than just one post – you should have a full campaign of what is running where (some groups find social media campaigns are best explained through a calendar that shows what types of posts will run when and then an executed post or two as an example). Remember, your campaign ideas and the one executed should embody your campaign theme.

EVALUTATION: Assuming that your entire campaign was executed, what would be the best way to evaluate its success – Would you conduct research? Measure sales? NOTE: You do not need to complete the evaluation, just describe in detail how you would evaluate it. Make sure you reference your objectives.

For this section, you will want to restate your objectives and then how you plan to evaluate them. This may include a research proposal. For example, if your campaign is to ‘increase sales by 20% among targeted consumers by May 5, 2020,” then you would evaluate it by saying “As mentioned above, our first objective was to….. we would evaluate our campaign in regards to this objective by…..” In the case of objectives that have to do with attitude shift, you will be proposing to conduct research to determine if that shift took place. This may relate back to your background research section as well. For example, “As mentioned above, we will have done a survey prior to the campaign….In order to evaluate…. Again….” Be as descriptive as possible in this section.

COMMENTARY:  Every strategic communication campaign has strengths and weaknesses. What do you think are some of the strengths and weaknesses to your campaign?

You will want to explain the strengths and weaknesses of the campaign. Be reflective and give this thought.

You may attend office hours for the professor and TA with questions.
