## Slide 7

Step 1: Determine your goal

Three Types of Campaign Goals:

Awareness:  Reach and Increasing Brand Awareness

Consideration: Traffic to a website, engagement, app installs, lead generation

Conversion: Conversion activities on your website such as completed an application, checked out the shopping cart, or drove traffic to a physical store or location.


## Slide 8

Step 2: Determine your audience

Through paid social ads you can reach general and specific audiences:

Broad example: All people ages 18-65+ living in the U.S.

Medium example: People interested in state parks and camping who live in Minnesota.

Specific example: Upload a list of the email addresses of your current customers to inform them about a new product offering.

## Slide 10

Custom and Lookalike Audiences

Custom audiences are audiences that allow you to upload a list of your customers or people who you have obtained an email address or phone number from.

The data is hashed so when using a custom audience, you can’t identify who these people are on an individual level but it’s a way for you to reach an audience that is already highly engaged with your brand.

Once you have created a custom audience, you can then expand those targeting efforts by creating a lookalike audience which takes the characteristics of the custom audience and aims to find user profiles that have those same characteristics.

Using this targeting, you can expect that some of these people will also be interested in hearing from your brand.

Within a campaign, it is worth testing a generic audience vs. these more specific ones to see which one responds better.

## Slide 12

Step 4: Determine your budget and schedule

You can run your first social media campaign for $25-50.

Broader targeting tends to be more affordable. It becomes more expensive to try and reach specific people such as a list of customers.

For a schedule, it’s important to determine when your potential audience is most engaged on social. For example, if promoting snowblowers, focus on the late fall/early winter buying seasons.

You can set your campaign to run during specific hours of the day but the algorithm knows when to target people.


## Slide 13

Step 5: Develop your creative

Creative is one of your toughest tasks as you compete with a user’s organic content.

There are two main types of creative: images or video.

Videos drive higher engagement but static images can drive higher traffic to your website.


## Slide 14

Step 5: Paid creative best practices

Creative should be designed to get your point across as quickly and simply as possible.

In 2017 on Facebook, the average time a user watched a Facebook video post was 10 seconds. Only 7 percent of videos were played with sound on.

Have one clear call to action.


## Slide 15

Types of advertisements

Videos: Show off product features, and draw people in with sound and motion.

Images: Drive people to destination websites or apps through high-quality visuals.

Facebook/Instagram Carousel: Showcase up to ten images or videos within a single ad, each with its own link. Best practice is 3-5 frames.

Lead Generation Ads: Lead ads help you collect info from people who are interested in your business. A lead ad is presented using an image or video and is followed by a lead form when the user engages with the ad.

Post engagement: You can boost an organic post to help it reach more people and/or drive increased engagement. Engagement being likes, comments, shares.

Follower ads: Promote your page to grow your page following.

Website clicks: Ads specifically optimized to drive traffic to your website.

App install ads: Ads specifically optimized to drive app installs.

## Slide 17

Step 7: Monitor your live campaign and optimize.

Once a campaign is live, give the creative and audiences a few days to settle in and start performing. Then depending on the length of your campaign, start evaluating the creative and audiences on a daily or weekly basis.

Look at what is performing best and try to understand why.

Is the copy better?

Are the visuals better?

Is this audience a better fit for your product?


## Slide 18

Step 7: Monitor your live campaign and optimize.

Turn off the lower performing creative and audiences so that more budget will be allocated to the higher performing creative.

Repeat this throughout the lifecycle of the campaign.

Monitor ad engagements: likes, retweets and especially comments.

Be prepared to provide customer service on unrelated topics.

Be prepared to potentially hide negative comments and turn off an ad if it seems to be getting a lot of negative attention.


## Slide 19

The importance of testing

Finding the campaign secret sauce takes time and testing.

Each campaign should have some element of test and learn whether that’s testing multiple audiences or multiple creative versions.

Only tweak one variable at a time.


## Slide 20

Step 8: Analyze and report your results

Once a campaign has ended, take time to analyze what worked and why.

Review your objective, audience and creative to see what you could have done differently.

Compare social media to other advertising channels to see what was most effective and cost efficient.
