## Slide 16

Website Design Best Practices

Display consistent branding, ensuring that the logo, website colors, tone of voice accurately reflect your brand.

Have a single, clear call-to-action.

On a webpage where you have more time than in a social media post, you can share more detail about the value of the offer and address any objections one might have to not taking the offer.

Provide intuitive navigation including clear language in your navigation menu, detailed navigation maps and breadcrumbs that let users easily return to a macrolevel page.


## Slide 17

Website Design Best Practices

Use clean design that takes advantage of white space. The easiest way to do this is to establish a visual hierarchy with the most important things having large headlines and more color/buttons.

Storytelling, visuals and testimonials: Start with good storytelling if that’s what makes your brand unique. Not every purchase website requires a story but having compelling imagery can suffice. Feature images of the products, employees, people using the products, etc. Just as people might use Google Reviews when choosing a brand, website testimonials allow an outside to endorse you and your brand.

Use mobile first and responsive design, design principles and tailor your website to a small screen with less manual typing.


## Slide 18

Website Design Best Practices – Accessibility

Definition: Accessibility means designing websites and applications that can be used by individuals who have visual, motor, auditory, speech, or cognitive disabilities.

Before you put your website live, factor in testing by people with disabilities on your staging website.

As you develop your website, use an accessibility auditing service such as a11y guidelines.


## Slide 19

Website accessibility considerations from reading

Use contrasting colors: People with visual impairments could find it difficult to discern text from a background color if there isn’t enough contrast.

Use more than color to communicate visual cues: You can use text labels or patterns so those with visual challenges can discern information. Other visual cues, like bold or underlined text or using shapes and different sizes, can also be effective ways to deliver your content.

Support keyboard navigation: People with motor disabilities, visual impairment, and other disabilities are often dependent on a keyboard to navigate content. You can “scroll” through interactive content, like links, buttons, and text fields, using the “Tab” button on a keyboard. It’s important to put key information into these interactive features and not just in your copy so that users with impairments can engage with your content.


## Slide 20

Website Design Best Practices

Prioritize search engine optimization including:

Header tags (H1) that feature important keywords

Putting title tags and meta descriptions on all of your pages

Using short, descriptive URLs with keywords

Getting your website featured on other, prominent websites. For example, the MSP Magazine Best Of awards.

Monitor your site speed through free tools and seeing if you have a high bounce rate.

Use heatmaps to understand where the mouse and eye go on your website using a 3rd party tool.


## Slide 21

Website Design Best Practices

A/B Test: deploy 2 versions of your website to determine which CTAs, visuals, etc. perform better. Only tweak one variable at a time to understand why the better performing site worked better.

Whether it’s Google Analytics or another measurement tool, there’s many resources out there that will allow you to use data-driven analytics to optimize your website.
